Q: Given that \(\sin \theta = \frac{a}{b}\), with \(\theta\) obtuse and \(a\) and \(b\) both positive, find an expression for \(\tan \theta\) in terms of \(a\) and \(b\).
S: Since \(\sin \theta = \frac{a}{b}\) and \(\cos \theta = -\frac{\sqrt{b^2 - a^2}}{b}\), \(\tan \theta = \frac{\sin \theta}{\cos \theta} = \frac{a/b}{-\sqrt{b^2-a^2}/b} = -\frac{a}{\sqrt{b^2 - a^2}}\).

y11a-6e-q12-sin
Q: If \(\tan \alpha = m\), where \(m > 0\), find the possible values of \(\sin \alpha\) in terms of \(m\). (Express as 'value or -value')
S: Given \(\tan \alpha = m\), the opposite side is \(m\) and the adjacent is \(1\). The hypotenuse is \(\sqrt{1 + m^2}\). Tangent is positive in Quadrants I and III, so sine is positive or negative: \(\sin \alpha = \pm \frac{m}{\sqrt{1 + m^2}}\).

y11a-6e-q12-sec
Q: If \(\tan \alpha = m\), where \(m > 0\), find the possible values of \(\sec \alpha\) in terms of \(m\).
S: Using the identity \(\sec^2 \alpha = 1 + \tan^2 \alpha = 1 + m^2\). Taking the square root gives \(\sec \alpha = \pm \sqrt{1 + m^2}\).

y11a-6e-q13a
Q: Prove the algebraic identity \((k^2 - 1)^2 + (2k)^2 = (k^2 + 1)^2\). Show all expansion steps.
S: LHS = k^4 - 2k^2 + 1 + 4k^2 = k^4 + 2k^2 + 1 = (k^2 + 1)^2 = RHS

y11a-6e-q13b-sin
Q: If \(\cos x = \frac{k^2 - 1}{k^2 + 1}\), where \(x\) is acute and \(k > 1\), find an expression for \(\sin x\) in terms of \(k\).
S: Since \(x\) is acute, \(\sin x\) is positive. Let the adjacent side be \(k^2 - 1\) and the hypotenuse be \(k^2 + 1\). From part (a), the opposite side is \(2k\). Thus, \(\sin x = \frac{\text{Opposite}}{\text{Hypotenuse}} = \frac{2k}{k^2 + 1}\).

y11a-6e-q13b-tan
Q: If \(\cos x = \frac{k^2 - 1}{k^2 + 1}\), where \(x\) is acute and \(k > 1\), find an expression for \(\tan x\) in terms of \(k\).
S: Since \(\sin x = \frac{2k}{k^2 + 1}\) and \(\cos x = \frac{k^2 - 1}{k^2 + 1}\), \(\tan x = \frac{2k}{k^2 - 1}\).
