# Chapter Profile: Year 11 Advanced Chapter 10 — Euler’s number

**Source:** User-provided *CambridgeMATHS NSW Stage 6 – Mathematics Advanced*, Year 11, 2025 edition, Chapter 10 (sections 10A–10C, printed pp. 381–396).

## Core concepts and section map

- **10A The exponential function base \(e\):** Euler’s number is approximately \(e=2.7183\). The natural exponential function is \(y=e^x\), and its derivative is itself: \(\frac{d}{dx}e^x=e^x\). Its graph is positive for all real x, passes through \((0,1)\), and has horizontal asymptote \(y=0\).
- **10B Transformations of exponential functions:** evaluate powers of \(e\) with a calculator when a decimal is requested; identify translations, reflections, and dilations of \(y=e^x\) by comparing the transformed equation and key graph features.
- **10C The logarithmic function base \(e\):** \(\ln x=\log_e x\) is the inverse of \(e^x\). Its domain is \(x>0\), range is all real numbers, graph passes through \((1,0)\), and it is the reflection of \(y=e^x\) in \(y=x\). Use inverse relationships \(\ln(e^x)=x\) and \(e^{\ln x}=x\) for \(x>0\).

## Standard solution method

1. Identify whether the task concerns an exponential value, a graph transformation, a logarithm, or an inverse relationship.
2. For graph questions, mark the parent graph’s anchor point and asymptote first; then apply each translation/reflection/dilation to both features.
3. For logarithmic equations, check the argument is positive before using logarithm laws or applying \(e^{(\cdot)}\) to both sides.
4. Use inverse identities to simplify, showing the substitution explicitly; preserve exact values unless a decimal is requested.
5. State domain and range restrictions alongside any final logarithmic expression.

## Common student pitfalls

- Treating \(e\) as a variable rather than a fixed number.
- Assuming \(e^x\) can be zero or negative; it is always positive.
- Forgetting that \(\ln x\) requires \(x>0\), including after solving an equation.
- Confusing \(\ln(e^x)\) with \(e^{\ln x}\) or omitting the condition on the latter.
- Applying a graph translation in the wrong direction or moving the asymptote when a transformation does not move it.
- Reporting a rounded calculator value when an exact answer is expected.
