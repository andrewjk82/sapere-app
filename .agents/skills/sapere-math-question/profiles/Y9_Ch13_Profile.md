# Chapter Profile: Year 9 Chapter 13 — Trigonometry

Source checked: *ICE-EM Mathematics Year 9 Book 2*, Chapter 13 (sections 13A–13G).

## Core concepts
- **13A Introduction:** similar right triangles have the same side ratios. Relative to the reference angle, identify opposite, adjacent, and hypotenuse.
- **13B Tangent:** τan θ = opposite / adjacent.
- **13C Basic ratios:** sin θ = opposite / hypotenuse; cos θ = adjacent / hypotenuse; tan θ = opposite / adjacent.
- **13D Finding angles:** rearrange the appropriate ratio and use its inverse; use degree mode and round as requested.
- **13F Applications:** model the context with a labelled right triangle; angles of elevation/depression are measured from the horizontal.
- **13G True bearings:** bearings are measured clockwise from north and written as three digits. Resolve a route into north/south and east/west components, respecting any stated total-distance or total-displacement condition.

## Solution method
1. Draw and label a right triangle or route diagram; identify the reference angle and the exact sides used.
2. Select sine, cosine, or tangent from the side pair, or set up the right-triangle components for a bearing.
3. Substitute the known values, rearrange, and calculate with units.
4. Round only at the end and check that the result is geometrically plausible.

## Common pitfalls
- Naming opposite/adjacent relative to the wrong angle.
- Using the hypotenuse as the adjacent side, or choosing a ratio before identifying the sides.
- Measuring an angle of depression from the sloping line instead of the horizontal.
- Measuring a bearing anticlockwise or from east; true bearings start at north and turn clockwise.
- Forgetting to use the inverse trigonometric function when the unknown is an angle.
- Rounding intermediate values too early.

## Diagram requirements
Every trigonometry figure must label the reference angle and all given/unknown sides. Angle arcs must sit in the sector between the exact rays used; elevation/depression arcs are drawn from the horizontal. A bearing arc starts at north and sweeps clockwise. In each solution stage, highlight the sides and angle used in that calculation.
