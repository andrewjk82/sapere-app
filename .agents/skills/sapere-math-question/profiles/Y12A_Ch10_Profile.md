# Chapter Profile: Year 12 Advanced Chapter 10 — Continuous probability distributions

## Core concepts
- A probability density satisfies \(f(x)\ge0\) and total area over its support equals 1.
- \(P(a<X<b)=\int_a^b f(x)dx\); a median m satisfies cumulative area 1/2.
- A mode maximises the density.
- Standardisation: \(z=(x-\mu)/\sigma\); normal-distribution areas use symmetry and the specified empirical rule/table.

## Standard methodology
1. State the support and normalization or cumulative-probability condition.
2. Integrate with correct bounds and solve for the constant, quantile, or probability.
3. For normal data, convert to z-scores, use symmetry/empirical rule, then convert back to original units.
4. For standard-normal quartiles/outliers, use symmetry for quartiles and apply the 1.5 IQR fences.

## Pitfalls
- A density value is not itself a probability; area is probability.
- A median splits total area in half, not the x-range in half.
- Empirical-rule percentages are approximate; preserve that qualification.
