# Chapter Profile: Year 10 Chapter 9
## Indices, exponentials and logarithms

### Core concepts
- Use (a^m a^n=a^{m+n}), (a^m/a^n=a^{m-n}), ((a^m)^n=a^{mn}), and (a^0=1) for (a\ne0).
- A negative index means a reciprocal: (a^{-n}=1/a^n). A fractional index means a root: (a^{m/n}=\sqrt[n]{a^m}) when defined.
- Rewrite expressions with a common base before combining exponents, and apply order of operations before simplifying constants.

### Common pitfalls
- Multiplying exponents when multiplying same-base powers instead of adding them.
- Applying a power to the coefficient but not every variable factor inside parentheses.
- Treating (a^0) as zero rather than one.
- Reversing numerator and denominator for a negative index or swapping numerator and denominator of a fractional index.
- Combining unlike bases before rewriting them as powers of the same base.

### Step-by-step method
1. Identify the index law and rewrite roots/reciprocals as powers if useful.
2. Expand powers of products across every factor.
3. Combine exponents only for matching bases, respecting order of operations.
4. Give the final expression in the requested form and check the sign of each exponent.
