## Core methods and explanation standards

- **Directed numbers:** evaluate brackets first and apply the sign immediately before each term. Two adjacent signs combine according to the integer sign rules.
- **Substitution:** replace every instance of the pronumeral with a bracketed value, then evaluate powers and operations in order. Brackets are essential for negative values.
- **Collecting like terms:** combine coefficients only when the variable part, including powers, is identical; retain unlike terms separately.
- **Expansion:** use the distributive law, multiplying every term in a bracket by every term in the other factor where required; collect like terms only after all products are written.
- **Common-factor factorisation:** take out the greatest common numerical and variable factor; verify by re-expanding.
- **Difference of squares:** identify (A^2-B^2) and apply (A^2-B^2=(A-B)(A+B)).
- **Grouping:** group terms so each group has a common factor, factor each group, then factor the shared bracket.
- **Quadratic trinomials:** find factor pairs of (ac) whose sum is (b), split the middle term, and factor by grouping; for monic trinomials, use factors whose product is (c) and sum is (b).
- **Algebraic fractions:** factor the numerator and denominator completely, cancel common factors, and retain the restriction that any original denominator cannot be zero when it is relevant to the question.

## Common pitfalls to address

- Treating subtraction of a negative as subtraction of a positive.
- Forgetting to substitute a negative value inside parentheses before squaring/cubing.
- Applying BODMAS in the wrong order or mishandling a leading negative sign.
- Combining terms with different variables or powers.
- Multiplying a bracket by only its first term or losing a negative sign.
- Factoring out a non-greatest factor and stopping early.
- Using (A^2-B^2=(A-B)^2) instead of the conjugate factors.
- Grouping into pairs that do not produce the same bracket.
- Choosing factor pairs with the correct product but wrong sum in a trinomial.
- Cancelling terms across addition/subtraction instead of cancelling common factors.
