# Chapter Profile: Year 10 Chapter 1
## Consumer arithmetic

Source: ICE-EM Mathematics 10 (3rd edition), Chapter 1, *Number and Algebra* — the chapter opens with Consumer arithmetic and covers percentage methods, commission, profit/loss, income tax and interest. This profile summarizes the methods needed for the Y10 Ch1 question bank.

### Core concepts and methods

- Convert a percentage to a multiplier: an increase of $p\\%$ uses $1+p/100$; a decrease uses $1-p/100$. To find $p\\%$ of an amount, multiply by $p/100$.
- Find what percentage one amount is of another with \(\frac{\text{part}}{\text{whole}}\times100\%\), first converting units if needed.
- Recover an original amount by dividing the known result by its multiplier. For example, after a discount of $d\\%$, divide the sale price by $1-d/100$; do not subtract the discount again.
- Percentage change is \(\frac{\text{new}-\text{original}}{\text{original}}\times100\%\). Keep the original amount as the denominator and state increase or decrease where requested.
- Apply successive percentage changes by multiplying their separate multipliers. A rise followed by a fall of the same percentage does not generally return to the starting value.
- For percentage composition or dilution, preserve the amount of the relevant substance: \(\text{solute amount}=\text{concentration}\times\text{total volume}\). Adding pure water changes total volume, not solute amount.
- Profit/loss percentage is calculated against cost price; selling price equals cost price multiplied by $1\\pm r/100$. For markup and later discount, apply each multiplier in sequence.
- Commission is a stated percentage of the relevant sales or transaction value. Identify the base amount before applying the rate.
- For progressive income tax, calculate each bracket separately using only the income within that bracket, then add the bracket taxes. Do not apply the top marginal rate to all income.
- Simple interest: \(I=Prt\), where \(r\) is a decimal and $t$ is the number of matching time periods. Amount owed/received is \(P+I\). Rearrange the same relation to find an unknown principal, rate, or time.
- Compound growth: \(A=P(1+r)^n\); compound depreciation: \(V=P(1-r)^n\). The exponent counts compounding periods. Interest earned is \(A-P\), while percentage growth/decrease compares the change with $P$.
- For regular deposits made at period end, compound each deposit for the number of periods it remains invested, then sum the values. Do not compound the final deposit for a full period when it is made at the end of that period.
- When the unknown is the number of compound periods or the rate, form the equation first, then use logarithms or roots as appropriate. For inflation-adjusted purchasing power, remove inflation growth from the nominal future value using the inflation multiplier.
- For comparison questions, calculate the total cost/return of each option on the same principal and time basis before comparing.

### Common pitfalls

- Using the new amount instead of the original as the percentage-change base.
- Treating a percentage change as an additive amount when it applies repeatedly.
- Reversing an increase/decrease with subtraction instead of division by the original multiplier.
- Applying progressive tax rates to the whole income instead of their individual bands.
- Mixing a percentage such as $6\\%$ with its decimal multiplier $0.06$, or using months as years without conversion.
- Confusing simple interest with compound interest, or reporting the final amount when asked for interest only.
- Rounding intermediate values too early; retain precision and round the final result as the context requests.

### Hint-writing guidance

For each question, highlight the shortest exact phrase that signals the key operation (such as the unknown, percentage change, repeated period, or tax band). Give one concise English hint that names the applicable relationship and tells the student what quantity to use as the base. Do not reveal the final numerical answer. For multi-part questions, attach hints to the individual parts when each part asks for a different operation.
