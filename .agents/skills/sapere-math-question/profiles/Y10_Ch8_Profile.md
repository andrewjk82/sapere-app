# Chapter Profile: Year 10 Chapter 8
## Review of congruence and similarity

### Core concepts
- In a square, adjacent sides are perpendicular and equal; the centre of an inscribed circle is one radius from each side.
- Apply Pythagoras only to a right triangle, identifying the perpendicular legs and hypotenuse before substituting.
- A circle radius is perpendicular to a tangent at the point of contact. For two externally tangent circles, the distance between centres is the sum of the radii.
- Keep geometric quantities attached to the correct points and segments throughout a multi-part problem.

### Common pitfalls
- Using the square's full side as a right-triangle leg when the centre-to-side distance is one radius.
- Confusing a small-circle radius with the distance between the circle centres.
- Omitting the larger circle's radius when using external tangency.
- Reusing an earlier result without showing its derivation in a separately opened sub-question.

### Step-by-step method
1. Mark the relevant right angle, radii, tangent points, and collinear centres on the diagram.
2. Highlight the exact sides used and state why the triangle is right-angled.
3. Substitute into Pythagoras or the tangency relation and show the algebra.
4. Simplify the exact value, retain units, and check that a length is positive and geometrically plausible.
