# Chapter Profile: Year 8 Chapter 8
## Pythagoras’ theorem and irrational numbers

Use ICE-EM Year 8 Book 1 sections 8A–8C. For a right-angled triangle, the square of the hypotenuse is the sum of the squares of the shorter sides. Identify the right angle first; the side opposite it is the hypotenuse. If working with coordinate points, the horizontal and vertical coordinate differences form the two perpendicular legs. Section 8C introduces irrational square roots and calculator approximations; do not assume advanced surd simplification or trigonometry is part of this chapter.

Solution flow: identify the right triangle and label its legs/hypotenuse; write the Pythagorean equation; substitute known lengths; solve and take the positive length; round only if requested. Common errors: using the hypotenuse as a shorter side, subtracting when finding a hypotenuse, and forgetting to square-root the final squared length.
