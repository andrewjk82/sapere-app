# Chapter Profile: Year 11 Advanced Chapter 11 — Radian measure of angles

Source: CambridgeMATHS NSW Stage 6 Mathematics Advanced, Chapter 11 (sections 11A–11C), user-provided textbook PDF.

## Core concepts and formulas
- One full revolution is \(2\pi\) radians, a straight angle is \(\pi\), and a right angle is \(\pi/2\).
- Radian measure is the ratio of arc length to radius and is dimensionless.
- Convert degrees to radians by multiplying by \(\pi/180\); convert radians to degrees by multiplying by \(180/\pi\).
- Arc length: \(s=r\theta\), with \(\theta\) in radians.
- Sector area: \(A=\frac12 r^2\theta\), with \(\theta\) in radians.
- When a sector is removed from a circle, the remaining arc length is the curved edge of the remaining paper. When its straight cut edges are joined to form a cone, that arc becomes the circumference of the cone base.

## Standard solution methodology
1. Identify whether the problem uses the full circle, a removed sector, or a remaining sector; calculate the correct angle in radians.
2. Identify the radius and the required arc or sector quantity.
3. Substitute into \(s=r\theta\) or \(A=\frac12r^2\theta\), showing units and exact arithmetic.
4. Interpret the arc in context (for example, as a cone-base circumference) and state the exact result.

## Common pitfalls
- Using the removed sector angle instead of the angle of the remaining paper.
- Treating degrees as radians in \(s=r\theta\) or the sector-area formula.
- Confusing radius with diameter, or reporting an arc length without connecting it to the requested circumference.
- Adding square units to a length or omitting units from the final result.
