# Chapter Profile: Year 10 Chapter 3
## Algebra review

### Core concepts
- Expand brackets using the distributive law, collect only like terms, and use identities such as the difference of two squares.
- Factorise by taking out a common factor first, then use grouping, quadratic factorisation, or difference of squares until every factor is irreducible over the intended number system.
- Simplify algebraic fractions by factoring numerator and denominator before cancelling common factors. For sums/differences, find a common denominator and combine numerators carefully.
- For quartics in even powers, substitute a temporary variable such as (u=t^2), factor the quadratic in (u), then factor any remaining difference of squares.

### Common pitfalls
- Cancelling terms across addition or subtraction instead of cancelling common factors.
- Losing a negative sign when factoring expressions such as (16-k^2).
- Expanding or factoring without checking the result by multiplication.
- Forgetting restrictions from original denominators when simplifying rational expressions.
- Stopping after a partial factorisation when the prompt says “fully”.

### Step-by-step method
1. Copy the expression and identify whether to expand, factor, or combine fractions.
2. Factor completely before cancelling; preserve denominator restrictions.
3. Show the intermediate numerator or product, including signs and coefficients.
4. Verify by expanding or substituting a simple permissible value, then give the simplified result.
