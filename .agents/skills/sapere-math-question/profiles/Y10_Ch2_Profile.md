# Chapter Profile: Year 10 Chapter 2
## Review of surds

### Core concepts
- A rational number has a terminating or recurring decimal expansion; an irrational number does not. Write recurring decimals as fractions when asked.
- Simplify surds by extracting the largest square factor, then combine only like surds.
- Multiply and divide surds using product and quotient rules; expand brackets term by term and collect like terms.
- Rationalise a monomial denominator by multiplying top and bottom by its surd. For a binomial denominator, use its conjugate and the difference-of-squares identity.

### Common pitfalls
- Adding radicands instead of coefficients of like surds.
- Leaving a square factor inside a radical or failing to factor out the greatest square factor.
- Expanding a squared binomial without the middle term.
- Multiplying by only part of a denominator or failing to use the conjugate for a binomial.
- Treating a recurring decimal as irrational or using the wrong recurring block.

### Step-by-step method
1. Identify the surd rule or recurring-decimal structure needed.
2. Factor radicands and simplify each root before combining terms.
3. Show each multiplication, expansion, or rationalising step explicitly.
4. State the exact simplified result and check that it matches the requested form.
