# Chapter Profile: Year 11 Advanced Chapter 7 — Lines in the coordinate plane

**Source:** User-provided *CambridgeMATHS NSW Stage 6 – Mathematics Advanced*, Year 11, 2025 edition, Chapter 7 (sections 7A–7E, printed pp. 239–263).

## Core concepts and section map

- **7A Lengths and midpoints of line segments:** for endpoints \(P(x_1,y_1)\) and \(Q(x_2,y_2)\), distance is \(PQ=\sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2+(y_2-y_1)^2}\); midpoint is \(M=(\frac{x_1+x_2}{2},\frac{y_1+y_2}{2})\).
- **7B Gradients of line segments and lines:** \(m=\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}\) when \(x_2\ne x_1\). Parallel lines have equal gradients; perpendicular non-vertical lines have gradients whose product is \(-1\). A vertical line has undefined gradient.
- **7C Equations of lines:** horizontal line through \((a,b)\) is \(y=b\); vertical line is \(x=a\). Use gradient–intercept form \(y=mx+c\), general form \(ax+by+c=0\), or point–gradient form as appropriate.
- **7D Further equations of lines:** use \(y-y_1=m(x-x_1)\) for a line with known gradient through a point; find the gradient from two points when needed; solve simultaneous line equations to find intersections and test concurrency.
- **7E Using pronumerals in place of numbers:** apply distance, midpoint, gradient, and line-equation formulae symbolically to prove general geometric results.

## Standard solution method

1. Record each point as an ordered pair and identify whether the task needs a distance, midpoint, gradient, equation, or intersection.
2. Substitute coordinates into the matching formula, keeping x-coordinates and y-coordinates in the correct positions.
3. For a line equation, find the gradient first if needed, then substitute a known point into point–gradient form and simplify.
4. For parallel/perpendicular claims, compare gradients explicitly. For intersections, solve the two line equations simultaneously and check the point satisfies both.
5. Give exact coordinates and simplify surds where possible.

## Common student pitfalls

- Reversing only one coordinate difference in the gradient or distance formula.
- Dividing by zero for a vertical line; state its equation as \(x=a\) instead.
- Treating a negative reciprocal as simply the negative gradient; for perpendicular lines use \(m_1m_2=-1\).
- Mixing the x- and y-coordinates in the midpoint formula.
- Using the right gradient but substituting the wrong point into the line equation.
- Rounding exact distances or coordinates when the question does not request an approximation.
