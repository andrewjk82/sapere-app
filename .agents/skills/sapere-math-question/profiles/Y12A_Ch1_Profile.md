# Chapter Profile: Year 12 Advanced Chapter 1
## Sequences and series

### Core concepts and formulas
- Arithmetic sequence: \(T_n=a+(n-1)d\), with common difference \(d\).
- Arithmetic series: \(S_n=\frac n2(2a+(n-1)d)=\frac n2(a+T_n)\).
- Geometric sequence: \(T_n=ar^{n-1}\), with common ratio \(r\).
- Finite geometric series: \(S_n=\frac{a(1-r^n)}{1-r}\), for \(r\ne1\).
- Infinite geometric series converges only when \(|r|<1\), with \(S_\infty=\frac a{1-r}\).
- If successive areas or lengths are powers of one scale factor, identify whether the series ratio applies to lengths or to squared lengths.
- For repeated event attempts with success probability \(p\) and failure probability \(q=1-p\), the first success on attempts \(1,2,3,\ldots\) has probabilities \(p, qp, q^2p,\ldots\).

### Standard solution progression
1. Identify whether the pattern is arithmetic (constant difference) or geometric (constant ratio), and record the first term and requested term/number of terms.
2. Convert contextual units before forming sequence terms or a sum.
3. Select the term or sum formula and substitute the known values with indices aligned carefully.
4. Solve the resulting equation, retain only values satisfying stated domain/positivity conditions, and interpret the result in context.
5. For an infinite sum, first verify \(|r|<1\); for repeated independent trials, distinguish an individual's turns from all turns.

### Common pitfalls
- Confusing a sequence term \(T_n\) with a partial sum \(S_n\).
- Counting \(T_m\) through \(T_n\) as \(n-m\) terms instead of \(n-m+1\).
- Using \(r\) rather than \(r^2\) when squaring geometric side lengths to obtain areas.
- Applying the infinite-series formula when \(|r|\ge1\).
- Adding percentage/proportional growth in a context where the growth increments themselves form a geometric sequence.
- Forgetting that one player's repeated attempts are separated by the opponent's failure turns.
