## Core concepts and standard methods

### Integers

- Place integers on a number line; values increase to the right. Among negative values, the one closer to zero is greater.
- Addition of a positive number moves right; subtraction of a positive number moves left. Subtracting a negative is equivalent to adding its positive opposite.
- For multiplication and division, equal signs give a positive result and unlike signs give a negative result. Zero multiplied by any integer is zero; division by zero is undefined.
- Evaluate brackets first, then powers, multiplication/division from left to right, and addition/subtraction from left to right.
- When solving a linear equation, keep it balanced by applying the same inverse operation to both sides, then check the value in the original equation.
- On the Cartesian plane, left/right changes the first coordinate and down/up changes the second. Quadrants are identified by the signs of the ordered pair.

### Fractions

- To compare fractions, rewrite them with a common denominator (often the LCM), then compare numerators.
- Add or subtract only after obtaining a common denominator. Simplify by dividing numerator and denominator by a common factor.
- Convert a mixed numeral to an improper fraction before multiplying or dividing mixed numerals. Convert back when the requested form is a mixed number.
- Multiply numerators and denominators; cancel common factors before or after multiplication.
- Divide by a fraction by multiplying by its reciprocal. Keep the dividend and reciprocal roles clear.
- For a fraction-of-a-sum expression, calculate the sum inside the brackets first, then multiply by the stated fraction.
- For a part-whole word problem, add known fractions, subtract from one to find the remaining fraction, then use that fraction with the supplied count to recover the whole.
- For HCF, take common prime factors with the smaller exponents. For LCM, take every prime factor with the larger exponents.

## Common student pitfalls to address

- Treating multiplication as having priority over division (or vice versa) instead of evaluating both left to right.
- Subtracting a negative without changing it to addition, or assuming the difference of two negative numbers has a fixed sign.
- Ordering negative numbers by the size of their digits rather than by their values.
- Applying a sign rule to a sum or difference instead of only to multiplication/division.
- Changing only one term when clearing denominators or comparing fractions.
- Adding or subtracting numerators and denominators directly.
- Multiplying mixed numerals without first converting them to improper fractions.
- Dividing fractions without taking the reciprocal of the divisor.
- Reporting a fraction before simplifying it when simplest form is requested.
- In a fraction-of-a-whole problem, using the fraction already accounted for instead of the unassigned remainder.
- On a translated point, applying the horizontal shift to the second coordinate or the vertical shift to the first.
