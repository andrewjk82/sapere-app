## Standard methods

### Decimal arithmetic and place value
- Align decimal points for addition and subtraction; add trailing zeroes when helpful.
- For multiplication, multiply as whole numbers, then place the decimal using the total number of decimal places in both factors.
- For division by a power of ten, move the decimal point left by the corresponding number of places. For division by a decimal, multiply dividend and divisor by the same power of ten to make the divisor an integer.
- For rounding, identify the requested place and inspect the next digit; 5–9 rounds up, 0–4 leaves the target digit unchanged.
- For recurring decimals, identify the repeating block and use a bar or ellipsis notation. For a fraction converted to a decimal to a stated accuracy, divide and round at the requested place.
- Compare decimals by writing equal-length decimal forms or comparing place values from left to right.
- Convert terminating decimals to fractions over a power of ten, then simplify by the highest common factor.
- In money contexts, keep units and decimal places clear. For “full” items, take the integer part of the quotient. For costs, multiply quantity by unit price.

### Geometry, angles and transformations
- A full turn is 360 degrees; a straight angle is 180 degrees; triangle interior angles sum to 180 degrees; quadrilateral interior angles sum to 360 degrees.
- The exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of its two opposite interior angles.
- Equal-side marks in an isosceles triangle identify equal opposite base angles. A linear pair sums to 180 degrees; angles around a point sum to 360 degrees.
- For a concave quadrilateral, use the interior angle sum of 360 degrees; distinguish a reflex interior angle from the smaller exterior angle.
- Reflection preserves distance perpendicular to the mirror line and reverses orientation. Rotation preserves distances from its centre. Apply transformations in the stated order.
- Rotational symmetry order is the number of times a shape matches itself in one full 360-degree turn.
- An axis of symmetry must divide a complete shape into matching mirror halves; ignore internal decoration unless the outline/marking makes it part of the shape.
- Drawing tasks should remain constructed-response tasks. Their solution diagrams belong in solution steps, not as a finished answer in the question prompt.

## Common pitfalls
- Misaligning decimal places, misplacing the decimal point in products, or moving it the wrong way in division.
- Rounding to the wrong place or inspecting the wrong digit; truncating instead of rounding.
- Confusing recurring digits, using a calculator-style rounded value as exact, or not simplifying a fraction.
- Returning a fractional number of whole glasses instead of the greatest whole number.
- Using quantity alone instead of multiplying by the unit price.
- Treating “less than 0.8 per container” as equality without checking which option could result.
- Confusing interior/exterior, adjacent/opposite, and reflex angles; forgetting the equal-side marks or parallel arrows.
- Reflecting across the wrong mirror line, rotating in the wrong direction/order, or counting the full turn incorrectly for rotational symmetry.
- Calling a visually approximate axis a symmetry axis without checking both halves.
